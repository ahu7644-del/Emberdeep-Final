# What If The World Had No Wind? (TikTok, 9:16)

A 2:06 vertical explainer in cinematic pixel art. Everything is generated
by code: the art, the HUD, the word-synced captions, the narration (local TTS),
the music and the sound effects.

**Output:** `out/no_wind_tiktok.mp4` (cover frame: `out/cover.png`) (1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264 + AAC, loudness-normalized to -14 LUFS)

## Narration script

> What if, tomorrow morning... the wind just **stopped?** Not slowed down. **Stopped.** Everywhere. **Forever.**
> Sounds peaceful, right? It's **not.** Here's how fast it all falls apart.
>
> **Hour one.** Every wind turbine on Earth stops spinning. That's over a **terawatt** of power, gone.
> Close to a **tenth** of the world's electricity. Denmark just lost more than **half** of its.
>
> **Day one.** The air over cities stops moving. Exhaust and smoke have nowhere to go, so they just **pile up.**
> In 1952, five days of dead-still air over London killed **thousands.** Now make that **permanent.** Everywhere.
>
> **Week one.** Weather stops moving. Storms don't roll in... or roll out. Where it's raining, it **keeps** raining.
> Where it's dry, it **stays** dry. Ocean moisture never makes it inland. So the monsoon that waters over a **billion**
> people... never comes. The good news? No hurricanes. No tornadoes. The bad news? We're just getting **started.**
>
> **Month one.** The oceans go glass-flat. No wind, no **waves.** Wind-driven currents, like the Gulf Stream, start to
> **fade.** And Europe loses the warm water that keeps its winters **mild.** Wind also drags cold, nutrient-rich water
> up from the deep. Those upwelling zones are under 1% of the ocean, but supply up to a **fifth** of all wild-caught
> fish. Shut them off, and the food chain starves from the **bottom up.**
>
> **Month three.** Corn, the most-grown grain on Earth, needs wind to carry its **pollen.** So do pines, oaks, and most
> grasses. Dandelion seeds go **nowhere.** Albatrosses, built to glide on the wind, sit **stranded** on the sea. And the
> Amazon? Every year, wind carries millions of tons of Sahara dust across the Atlantic to **fertilize** it. That
> delivery? **Cancelled.**
>
> **Year one.** Wind is Earth's air conditioner, hauling heat from the equator toward the poles. Switch it off, and the
> tropics **bake,** while the poles freeze even **harder.** Far from the coasts, green land dries into **desert.**
>
> So... who keeps the lights **on?** Solar still works. But with no breeze to cool them, panels run hot and lose
> **power.** Hydropower fades, as rain stops reaching the mountains. The surprise winner? **Tidal power.** Because the
> Moon doesn't need **wind.**
>
> Wind is just the planet **breathing.** We never notice it... until it's **gone.** Follow for the next **what if.**

## Fact notes (what the claims rest on)

- **Wind share of electricity.** Wind supplied about 8% of global electricity in 2024 (Ember Global Electricity Review). Installed capacity passed 1 TW in 2023 (GWEC). Denmark gets more than half of its electricity from wind.
- **London 1952.** The Great Smog lasted 5 to 9 December 1952, under windless anticyclonic conditions. About 4,000 people died at the time, and later estimates run to 10,000–12,000.
- **Monsoon.** The South Asian monsoon provides most of the annual rain for well over a billion people.
- **Waves and currents.** Ocean waves are made by wind. The big surface gyres, including the Gulf Stream, are largely wind-driven, and the Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift help keep northwest Europe's winters mild.
- **Upwelling.** Coastal (eastern-boundary) upwelling systems cover under 1% of the ocean but produce roughly 20% of the global wild fish catch.
- **Pollination.** Maize/corn (the world's largest cereal crop by tonnage) is wind-pollinated, as are pines, oaks and most grasses.
- **Albatrosses** rely on dynamic soaring in wind. In calms they sit on the water. The wandering albatross has a wingspan of up to about 3.5 m.
- **Sahara to Amazon dust.** NASA CALIPSO estimates about 27.7 million tons a year of Saharan dust settle on the Amazon basin, carrying phosphorus.
- **Heat transport.** The atmosphere carries much of Earth's equator-to-pole heat transport.
- **Solar panels** lose roughly 0.3–0.5% of their output per °C. Wind cooling lowers panel temperature.
- **Tides** are driven by the Moon's (and Sun's) gravity, not by wind.

This is a thought experiment. A truly windless atmosphere isn't physically possible on a planet heated unevenly by the Sun, which is the whole point of the video's closing line.

## Re-rendering

Requirements: Python 3.10+, `ffmpeg`, plus:

```bash
pip install numpy pillow scipy soundfile kokoro-onnx
```

Download the Kokoro TTS model (Apache-2.0) into `assets/kokoro/` (or point `KOKORO_DIR` at it):

```bash
mkdir -p assets/kokoro && cd assets/kokoro
curl -LO https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases/download/model-files-v1.0/kokoro-v1.0.onnx
curl -LO https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases/download/model-files-v1.0/voices-v1.0.bin
```

Then render:

```bash
python make_video.py                 # full render -> out/no_wind_tiktok.mp4 (~2.5 min on 4 cores)
python make_video.py --preview 2     # contact sheet every 2 s -> build/preview.png
python make_video.py --frames 3 41.5 # single full-res frames -> build/frame_*.png
VOICE=am_michael SPEED=1.15 python make_video.py   # try a different narrator voice / pace
```

### Files

- `make_video.py`: script, TTS, word timing, captions, HUD, frame loop, audio mix, encode
- `scenes.py`: the 25 shot renderers (meadow, wind farm, smog city, London 1952, globes, ocean, upwelling, corn, dandelion, albatross, solar, dam, tidal…)
- `pixelkit.py`: a small numpy pixel-art toolkit (Bayer-dithered gradients and transparency, shapes, sprites, and an orthographic pixel globe)
- `audio.py`: procedural D-minor score that adds layers as the chapters escalate, plus SFX (wind, sub-drop, risers, booms, rain, thunder, glitches, 8-bit blips)
