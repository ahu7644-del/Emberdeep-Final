Hot aisleThe problem

One micro-hotspot. A whole facility overcooled.

Data centers running high-density AI training workloads develop localized micro-hotspots. To protect expensive GPUs, operators aggressively overcool the entire facility, wasting massive amounts of electricity.

Who has the problem, and when it hits

Who
Data center operators, cloud service providers and enterprise AI infrastructure managers.AWS · Azure · Google Cloud · Equinix
When and where
Inside server racks and server rooms at data centers worldwide, during heavy AI model training runs and peak compute processing.
Why it matters
Cooling is roughly 40% of a data center’s electricity bill. Overcooling wastes millions of dollars per facility, strains electrical grids and increases carbon emissions.

What happens if nobody fixes it

As AI adoption expands, each of these gets worse.

  1. Costs skyrocket

    Operational costs climb with every new GPU cluster.

  2. Servers throttle

    Overheated GPUs slow themselves down.

  3. Hardware wears out early

    Heat shortens the life of expensive GPUs.

  4. Blackouts become possible

    Peak demand strains local grids.

  5. Sustainability targets slip

    Wasted energy means more emissions.
